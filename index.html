HECTOR Historical Economic Commodities: Terminologies, Ontologies & Rates
Alpha. Published for discussion, not yet for citation: records, identifiers and URIs may change or be withdrawn until the first full release. Comments are welcome as GitHub issues.

An open Linked Data vocabulary of historical traded commodities, the units they were measured and packed in, and the customs rates charged on them. Every record gathers the spellings the sources actually use, links to the Getty Art & Architecture Thesaurus and Wikidata where an equivalent exists, and is published as JSON-LD aligned with Linked Art. HECTOR is meant to grow to the goods of any trade, period and language; its first content comes from the London customs accounts (1380–1560) and the English Books of Rates of 1507, 1545 and 1558.

    Using the data

    • Every record has a URI, https://w3id.org/hector/commodity/saffron: a browser gets this page; ask for JSON and you get the record: 
      curl -L -H "Accept: application/ld+json" https://w3id.org/hector/commodity/saffron
    • JSON-LD context: https://w3id.org/hector/context, layered on the Linked Art context.
    • Vocabulary of HECTOR’s own terms: https://w3id.org/hector/ontology.
    • Slug ledgers (how each URI was minted, and what replaced it): commodities, qualified (withdrawn), units.
    • Every record also as Turtle and RDF/XML, beside its JSON-LD (…/ontology.ttl, …/ontology.rdf), and all of HECTOR in one file: hector.ttl.gz (Turtle, gzipped).
    • Everything, with the code that builds it: github.com/ihr-digital/hector.

    Not yet

    • Only an alpha pre-release (v2026.10-alpha.1), with no DOI: please do not cite the records or URIs.
    • The URIs negotiate JSON-LD only; Turtle and RDF/XML are files to fetch directly, until the w3id redirect rules are extended.
    • Rates beyond 1507–1558 (the 1604 book) are parsed but not yet published.
    • Commodities from other trades, places and languages: HECTOR is built to take them, and proposals for new sources are welcome as issues.

    Units are deliberately not linked to QUDT or other vocabularies of modern units: those define today's standard units, whereas historical units varied from place to place and over time, so a link would assert an equivalence the sources do not support. Each unit gives its own sourced definitions and conversions instead.
